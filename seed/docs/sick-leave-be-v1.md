# Sick-leave reporting — Belgium

## Purpose
This guidance tells consultants how sickness absences of Belgian client employees must be reported, documented and processed so that guaranteed salary is paid correctly and the health-insurance fund (mutualité / ziekenfonds) takes over on time.

## Who it applies to
All white-collar and blue-collar employees on a Belgian payroll. Specific sector rules may add stricter reporting steps; the client's work regulations always prevail when stricter.

## Rules
- The employee must notify the employer immediately, and at the latest within 24 hours of the start of the absence.
- A medical certificate must be delivered within 2 working days, unless the work regulations allow a certificate-free day. Employers with fewer than 50 employees may still require a certificate from day one.
- Up to three certificate-free sick days per calendar year are allowed, one at a time.
- Guaranteed salary is paid by the employer for the first 30 days (white-collar scheme). After that, the employee is declared to the health-insurance fund.

## How to process in payroll
Register the absence in the time module with absence code SICK-01 (guaranteed salary) or SICK-02 (certificate-free day). Absences longer than 30 days must be declared through the social-risk declaration (scenario 1) within 8 calendar days after the guaranteed-salary period ends.

## Exceptions
Relapses within 14 days of returning to work count as the same illness and do not open a new guaranteed-salary period. Contact the HR owner when an employee refuses a control doctor visit.

## Key rules
- Notification deadline: within 24 hours of the start of absence
- Medical certificate deadline: within 2 working days
- Certificate-free days: 3 per calendar year
- Guaranteed salary period: 30 days
- Absence code: SICK-01
